I have been the only girl on my school's robotics team for three years. There are eleven of us. I want to be careful about how I say this, because the story people expect is one where the boys were cruel to me and I overcame it, and that is not what happened. Most of them are fine. Two of them are my closest friends.

What happened was smaller and harder to point at, and the thing that fixed it was a habit I started for a completely different reason.

In my first year I built a lot and said almost nothing. During design meetings I would have an idea, and I would run it through my head four or five times looking for the flaw, and by the time I was sure it was good, somebody else had said something and the room had moved on. This happened constantly. I told myself it was because I was younger. Then the next year came and I was not younger and it kept happening.

The habit was this: I started writing my idea down before the meeting. Not a whole proposal. One line, on the back of whatever paper was near me, in the ten minutes between last period and when we met in the tech room. Just the sentence I would say if I said it.

I started doing it because I was forgetting things. That is honestly the whole reason. I would think of something at lunch and lose it by four o'clock. The note was a memory fix.

But having the sentence already written changed something I did not expect. When you have already decided the words, you do not have to build them in the two seconds you have. You just read them off. I stopped needing the perfect gap in the conversation. I could put the line down in an imperfect gap and it would still hold, because it was a finished sentence and not a half-formed one.

The first time it clearly mattered was our second competition that year. We had a chassis that kept losing the game piece on the turn, and everybody was arguing about the intake wheels, and my note that day said: the problem is the turn, not the grip, we are turning too fast because the driver can't see the far side. Nine words on the back of a chemistry worksheet. I read it out. Somebody said huh. We slowed the turn profile and the problem went away.

I do not think that note made me a better engineer. I already had the idea; that was never the missing part. What it did was close the gap between having the thought and the thought existing in the room, and I have come to believe that gap is where most of what I know has gone to die over the years.

I still do it. There is a stack of small papers in my bag right now, some of them from meetings that happened months ago, most of them saying things that turned out to be wrong. It does not matter that they were wrong. What matters is that they got said, and that I stopped being a person who was only correct silently.
