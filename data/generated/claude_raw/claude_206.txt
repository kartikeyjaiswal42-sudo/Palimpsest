I still have the microphone. It's sitting on a shelf in my room next to some books and a plant that's half-dead. I spent forty dollars on it after I decided to start a podcast, convinced that good equipment was the thing that was going to make my ideas matter. Looking back, that was the most optimistic I've ever been about anything.

The podcast was called "Half-Asleep Philosophy" because I thought the name was clever. The concept was that I would just talk about philosophical problems while sitting in my dark room late at night, the way you do when you can't sleep and your brain won't stop thinking about things like whether free will actually exists or if time is real. I recorded the intro probably fifteen times trying to get the right tone—casual but smart, conversational but intellectual. By take twelve I'd basically recorded the same thing four times and decided it was good enough.

The first episode was about Schrodinger's cat. I explained the paradox, talked about what it means for quantum states, rambled for about forty minutes about how this had changed the way I thought about uncertainty in my own life. I uploaded it to every podcast platform I could find and then I waited. I sent the link to my friends. My mom listened to it and said it was interesting but maybe a little hard to follow. One of my friends actually sent me a note saying she liked it, which felt like validation enough to keep going.

Episodes two and three came out every other week. They were about the ship of Theseus and then about whether AI could ever really be conscious. I spent hours researching before each recording. I rewrote scripts and then basically threw them out and just talked anyway. The audio quality was fine but not great. There were background noises—my fan, the ambient traffic from outside, that one time my mom knocked on my door. But I was getting maybe thirty downloads per episode, which didn't seem like nothing.

By the time I sat down to record episode four, something had shifted. I realized I was doing this performance of being the kind of person who has good ideas about philosophy, rather than actually exploring ideas. I was trying to sound smart instead of thinking clearly. The microphone in front of me looked less like a tool and more like a barrier.

I recorded episode four anyway. It was about determinism and it was the worst one. I was distracted the whole time. My thoughts were scattered. I published it and then I stopped. I told myself I was just taking a break, but I never went back.

Looking at that microphone now, I don't feel like I failed exactly. I made something that nobody asked me to make, for no money, with basically no audience, and I did it anyway. But I also learned that I was doing it for the wrong reasons. I was chasing the idea of being a podcaster instead of actually caring about the exploration. I wanted the credibility without the substance.

I still think about those philosophical problems sometimes. I still stay up late overthinking things. But now when I do, I'm not trying to package it into something. I'm just thinking. And I think that's actually fine.
