The shelf was in the young adult section of the library, middle shelf on one of the back racks where nobody usually went. I was fourteen and I had declared I would read every book on that shelf that summer. All of them. Consecutive. Like it was a quest.

I don't remember exactly why this shelf. I think I just looked at the library one day and saw it as a challenge. The shelf had maybe thirty books, mostly contemporary YA fiction. I thought it would be easy and fun. I thought by the end of the summer I would have this accomplishment, this complete thing I had finished.

By August I had read seventeen. Some of the books I loved. Some I found tedious. Some had endings that frustrated me. I kept going. I told myself I would finish the shelf. That's what I had promised myself I would do.

But then I hit a slump. There were three books in a row that I didn't like, and after finishing the third one, I couldn't make myself pick up the fourth. I had been reading for weeks and I was tired of it. I wasn't reading for pleasure anymore. I was reading because I had said I would read every book. The shelf had become an obligation.

I failed. I just stopped reading from that shelf. I didn't finish it. Thirteen books sat there unread. I felt like I had failed at something self-imposed, which was worse than failing at something someone else imposed on me. I had set the goal and I couldn't complete it.

For a while I felt bad about this. The library shelf became a kind of symbol of my inability to follow through on things. Every time I went to the library I would see that shelf and feel the failure of it. But then something else happened. I realized I had read seventeen books that summer. That's more reading than I had done in a long time. The failure of not finishing meant I missed thirteen books, but I had done something good anyway.

I went back to that shelf later. Not to finish the project the same way. Just to actually read the books I had missed, but on my own terms. I didn't feel like I had to read them consecutively. I didn't feel like I had to finish them if I didn't like them. I just read the ones that interested me.

What I did next is I learned something about completion and about failure. Sometimes failure is actually just a different kind of success. I failed to read the shelf, but I succeeded in reading seventeen books. That's not nothing. Also, I learned that self-imposed restrictions can become their own kind of prison. Reading the shelf had felt like freedom at first and then like work. True reading means changing your mind about what you're reading if you want to.

The shelf is still there. I still see it sometimes when I go to the library. I never did read every book on it. But I read enough of them to understand why I stopped, and I've learned to be okay with that. Failure taught me something that success couldn't have. It taught me that finishing everything isn't the point. The point is being honest about what you actually want to be reading, even if it means disappointing yourself on the shelf itself.
