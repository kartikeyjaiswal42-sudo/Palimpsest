"You didn't even read the rubric," Priya said, not looking up from her laptop, and I said something back I don't fully remember except that it ended with her snapping the laptop shut harder than she needed to.

That was ninth grade, the invasive species project, and it was the end of something that had actually started three years earlier over a much smaller argument — whether pineapple belonged on pizza. We decided, with great seriousness, at her eleventh birthday party, that it did. From then on, every Friday, one of us texted the other a single word. Pineapple. No context, no greeting, nothing before or after it. If I forgot, she sent it twice the next week, doubled, like a fine I owed her.

It sounds like nothing, and for a long time I thought it was nothing, just a dumb inside joke neither of us had the heart to retire. But it went on for three years without a single missed Friday, through her parents' divorce, through the year I didn't talk to anyone at lunch, through two different schools for her and one very bad summer for me. It was never a conversation. It was just proof, delivered weekly, that the other person was still there and still thinking of you, even when everything else you might have said felt too big to say.

The project came in April of ninth grade. We were assigned to the same group along with two people neither of us knew well, and somewhere in the second week I stopped doing my share and let her carry it, telling myself I was busy, telling myself she was better at this kind of thing anyway, which was true and also not an excuse. She noticed. She said something about it in front of the group, not cruelly, just tired, and I got defensive in the specific way people get defensive when they know they're wrong, and it turned into the kind of fight that isn't really about the thing it's about.

We finished the project. We got a decent grade. We did not speak for the rest of that year, not really, not beyond what the hallway required.

The Fridays didn't stop right away, which is the part I still think about. For three more weeks, I texted her pineapple, and she texted it back, one word passing between two people who otherwise had nothing left to say to each other. I don't know if she kept sending it out of habit or because some smaller, stubborn part of her wasn't ready to let the whole thing go either. Eventually one of us stopped — I genuinely don't remember which — and neither of us restarted it, and that was that.

We're civil now, in the way people are civil about things that used to matter more. She's dating someone I don't know. I run track with someone who used to be her best friend. Sometimes I still think, on a Friday, unprompted, of a single word, and I don't send it, and I couldn't tell you exactly why I don't, except that some habits only mean something as long as both people are still choosing them, on purpose, every single week, without being asked.
