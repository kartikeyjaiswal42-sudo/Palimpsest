For two years I didn't tell anyone at school that my brother has type 1 diabetes, mostly because I'd decided, without really deciding, that it was ours to handle and nobody else's business. My parents worked long shifts. I was the one home when Eli's blood sugar dropped in the afternoons, the one who learned to read the shakiness in his voice on the phone and know, before he said it, that I needed to grab juice and run. I got good at it. I also got quietly furious about it, in a way I didn't let myself look at directly.

The night that changed was not dramatic, which is annoying to admit because it would make a better essay if it were. We were at a Saturday clinic thing my mom finally dragged us to, a family support group at the children's hospital, mostly parents talking in one room while a nurse ran activities for younger siblings in another. I was seventeen, way older than the activity was meant for, and I sat in the back not participating, arms crossed, waiting for it to be over.

Then a girl next to me, maybe twelve, started talking about how she carb-counts her little sister's lunch every morning before school, unprompted, to a room of strangers, like it was just a normal Tuesday fact about her life. And I felt something crack a little, hearing someone else say out loud the exact math I did in my head every day and had never said to anyone. Not because her situation was identical to mine. Because she wasn't embarrassed by it, and I realized I'd been carrying mine like a secret I was ashamed of instead of something I could just say.

I stayed after and talked to her mom, then to mine, in the parking lot, longer than we'd planned to. That's the whole turn, really — not a decision to stop helping Eli, I never stopped, I still check his levels most nights. It was realizing I'd built a wall around it, telling myself it was private when it was actually just lonely, and that there were other kids driving their own siblings to appointments and keeping the same silent spreadsheets I kept.

I go back to that clinic group most months now, not because I need the carb-counting tips anymore, I've got those down, but because it's the one room where I don't have to explain the math before I can talk about anything real. My place there isn't the newest member or the most experienced one. It's more like the kid who shows up and, some Saturdays, is the one who finally says the thing someone else in the back row hasn't said yet.
