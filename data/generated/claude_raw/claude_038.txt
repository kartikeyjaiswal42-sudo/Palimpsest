I don't know how to explain to my parents what I feel in the sitting room on Thursday afternoons when I'm supposed to be watching my brother. It's not anger exactly, and it's not love exactly either. It's something sharper and more confused than either of those things, and I've been angry at them both for making me feel this way, for being the kind of family where I have to feel this way at all.

My brother has lupus. He was diagnosed in eighth grade, and now he's in tenth grade, and the disease is still the third person in our house, the one who gets the most attention even when we're not talking about it. His medication has to be taken at specific times. His appointments have to be scheduled around his energy levels. When he has a flare, everything in our family reorganizes itself around the fact of his suffering, and sometimes—I know this is terrible, I know this is wrong—I resent him for it.

On Thursday afternoons, I sit in the sitting room while he rests upstairs, and I'm supposed to be available if he needs something. I'm sixteen years old, which is old enough to understand that resentment toward a sick sibling is the kind of thing good people don't feel, but young enough to feel it anyway, acutely and without any way to process it. He didn't choose to be sick. I understand that. But he also gets to rest while I sit here, and he gets to miss school without it being a reflection on his character, and he gets to be tired in a way that's acceptable, whereas my tiredness from taking care of him is something I'm supposed to just absorb quietly.

My parents think I'm noble for helping. My mom has actually used that word. She said, "You're so noble, the way you look after your brother." And all I could think was how I didn't feel noble at all. I felt trapped and resentful and like I was being made into a better person against my will, which isn't nobility, it's just coercion dressed up in love.

The thing I don't tell anyone is that I love him. I love him so much that sometimes when he's having a flare and lying in bed in actual pain, I feel guilty for the resentment I've carried around, and that guilt makes me even angrier because now I'm angry at myself for being angry, and there's nowhere for any of this to go. He's my brother. He didn't make himself sick. He didn't make me be born first, or older, or more able-bodied. He didn't decide that our family would structure itself around his illness.

But he benefits from the fact that it did, and I think we both know that, and I think that's what I'm really angry about. Not that he's sick. Not that he needs help. But that the help I give him is treated as this beautiful act of sibling love rather than as labor, and labor that I didn't consent to perform. I love him. I also didn't choose this.

I don't know how to reconcile those two things. And I don't know if my parents have ever stopped long enough to ask me how I'm doing in all of this, or if they assume that love is enough to carry me through. Maybe it is. Maybe I'm being selfish and cruel for feeling the way I feel. Or maybe it's possible to love someone and still resent the situation that love has placed you in, and maybe nobody ever talks about that because it makes us all look less noble than we want to be.
