I have sung in my church's choir every Sunday for four years, and I have not believed in God for almost three of them. I've never told my grandmother this, and I don't know if I ever will, which is its own kind of unresolved argument, the kind that happens entirely in my head because I've never had the nerve to have it out loud.

The choir is a real community, not the vague kind people mean when they use that word in an essay. It's Mrs. Adeyemi, our director, who has no patience for sopranos who rush the tempo. It's the two Nguyen brothers, one bass and one tenor, who argue about basketball during every rehearsal break. It's the smell of the church basement, coffee and old carpet, and eight-part harmony landing exactly right after weeks of it not quite landing. I know these people better than I know most of my classmates.

What I don't have anymore is the belief that supposedly holds the whole thing together. It slipped away gradually, somewhere in tenth grade, through science classes and just thinking too hard during sermons. I didn't quit the choir when it happened. Some of it is honest — I love the music itself, independent of whatever it's supposedly praising. Some of it, if I'm being fully honest, is cowardice. Quitting would mean a conversation with my grandmother that I have rehearsed and abandoned probably twenty times.

My grandmother is the one I love and disagree with here, though we've never actually had the argument. She got me into the choir when I was ten, and she still watches from the third pew every Sunday with an expression I can only describe as complete faith, not just in God but in me. I know that if I told her the truth, she wouldn't yell. She'd be hurt, quietly, in a way that would be worse than yelling. So instead I sing the words and let her assume they mean to me what they mean to her, and I hate that this feels like the kinder choice, because it also feels like a lie I tell her every week without saying anything false out loud.

My place in this community is complicated in a way I haven't resolved for myself. I am fully present in the choir loft and fully absent from the belief that supposedly explains why I'm standing there. I don't know if that makes me a fraud or just someone who found a community worth keeping after losing the reason he first joined it. Some Sundays I think it's the second thing. Other Sundays, watching my grandmother mouth the words beside me, I think it might be the first.
