My dad thinks the pallet furniture thing is a phase, and I've stopped trying to convince him otherwise, mostly because I don't think I'll win, and partly because some stubborn part of me likes that we still haven't settled it.

It started two summers ago behind the hardware store where I worked weekends, where a stack of broken shipping pallets sat waiting for the scrap truck. I asked the manager if I could take a few. He shrugged and said sure, they were trash anyway, and I dragged three of them home on a hand truck and had no real plan beyond the vague sense that I could turn them into something.

The first thing I built was a coffee table, and it was bad — uneven, one leg shorter than the others, splinters everywhere because I hadn't sanded properly. My dad looked at it in the garage and said, not unkindly but not encouragingly either, that I could buy a table at the store for less than what the sandpaper and stain had cost me. He wasn't wrong about the money. He's an accountant; he's rarely wrong about the money. But that wasn't really what the table was for, and I couldn't explain that to him in a way that landed, then or really since.

I kept building anyway. A bench for the porch. A shelf unit that actually turned out decent, that my mom uses for her plants now. A headboard I gave my sister for her birthday, which she still has, three years later, in her dorm room a thousand miles from home. Each one got a little better, a little less crooked, and I got faster at seeing which broken slats were still usable and which ones needed to go straight to the scrap pile. My dad watched all of this from a distance, mostly approving of the results and mostly still unconvinced of the premise. He still says, every few months, some version of "you know you could just buy furniture," and I still say some version of "that's not the point," and neither of us has ever really finished that argument. We just set it down and pick it back up later, like a bookmark neither of us wants to lose.

I think what he doesn't fully get, and what I've never managed to say right, is that I'm not building furniture because I need furniture. I'm building it because there's a specific, quiet satisfaction in taking something the world has already decided is garbage and proving, with my own hands, that it wasn't finished being useful. A pallet gets thrown away because it did one job — carrying boxes across a warehouse floor — and everyone assumes the job was the whole point of it existing. I don't think that's true of pallets, and if I'm honest, some of why I keep doing this is that I don't think it's true of a lot of things people write off too fast, including, some days, myself.

I haven't said that to my dad in exactly those words. Maybe I will eventually. For now the garage still smells like sawdust most Saturdays, and he still stops by to look at whatever's half-finished on the workbench, and shakes his head a little, and doesn't leave. That's its own kind of answer, even if neither of us has said the thing out loud yet.
