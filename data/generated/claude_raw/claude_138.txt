My mom has not really forgiven me, and I don't think she ever will, which is the point I'm trying to explain here.

I stopped speaking Spanish at home when I was fourteen, right around when I started caring about fitting in at school. Everyone else spoke English. Their parents spoke English. Nobody had to think about what language to use—it was just the default, the neutral option, the American one. Spanish was the thing that marked you as different, and I was tired of being marked.

It started small. I'd answer my mom in English when she spoke to me in Spanish, and she'd sigh but repeat herself in English so she could get a response. I'd text my younger brother in English and he'd text back in English, and eventually we'd forgotten how to have certain conversations in Spanish because we were doing it all in English now. My dad had always been more English anyway, so he didn't care. But my mom cared.

We fought about it exactly once, clearly, when I was fifteen. She said I was forgetting where I came from and I said I wasn't forgetting anything, I was just choosing who I wanted to be. I remember her face when I said that. She looked like I'd told her I was choosing to erase her. I didn't mean it that way, but I also kind of did. I meant that I was choosing to not be the kid whose mom sent her to school with weird food and whose last name didn't fit the way other last names fit. I was choosing to just be American, which is what I was, mostly, anyway.

She stopped trying after that. She spoke to me in English even though I could see it cost her something, some small humiliation of having to accommodate her own child. My little brother kept speaking Spanish at home, and I'd listen to them talk and feel this weird mix of being outside the family and also being exactly where I wanted to be.

Three years later I'm taking Spanish classes again at school, and I'm angry about how hard it is to remember something I used to speak fluently. I'm angry that I let myself become the kind of person who needs to take a class to speak the language my mother used to sing me lullabies in. And I'm angry at my mom for not fighting harder to keep me speaking it, even though I know that's completely unfair. She tried. I refused. I was the one who shut the door.

I've tried talking to her about it since then. I've tried apologizing. She says there's nothing to apologize for, which is how I know she hasn't forgiven me. She says it very gently, like I'm still the person I was then, the one who wanted to escape her too much to care about our language. And maybe she's right. Maybe something in that choice was permanent.

My Spanish is getting better. It's coming back slower than I expected. But the thing that's hard isn't the grammar or the vocabulary. It's knowing that my mom is speaking English to me now because I taught her to, and that I can't really teach her back.
