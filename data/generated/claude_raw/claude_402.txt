The place where I feel most myself is my bedroom with the door closed and my supplies spread out in front of me. I make jewelry, mostly rings and bracelets from wire and beads. I've been doing it for years, and it's the only thing that quiets my mind when everything else is loud. But I did it badly once, which is something I still think about.

I decided to sell what I made. This seemed natural. I was making beautiful things. People complimented them. Why shouldn't I make money from something I was already doing? I set up an online shop and started listing my work. I was confident because I had been making jewelry for so long that I thought I knew what I was doing.

What I was wrong about was the difference between making something for yourself and making something for someone else. When you make something for yourself, you know all the details. You know the wire is sturdy. You know the clasp will hold. You know how long it lasts because you've worn it. When you make something to sell, you have to guarantee these things about every piece, and I didn't really understand that yet.

I made the products, but I didn't test them properly. I sold a bracelet to someone and she wore it twice and the clasp broke. I sold a ring and the wire loosened over time. I sold earrings and the hooks bent. Every issue would result in someone messaging me angry, and I would feel terrible, but I would also feel defensive. These were handmade. Some imperfection was natural.

The problem is that wasn't fair to the people buying from me. They paid money for these things. They expected them to work. My personal standards for my own jewelry were lower than the standards I should have had for something I was selling. I was in my bedroom with my supplies, but I wasn't treating it like a real business. I was treating it like playing.

What I did after this realization was I stopped selling online. I went back to making jewelry just for myself and for friends as gifts. But I changed how I made it. I tested everything twice before considering it done. I upgraded my materials. I took the time to understand what makes jewelry durable versus what makes it pretty. I spent weeks on this education, treating it seriously.

The place where I feel most myself is still my bedroom, still surrounded by my supplies. But now when I make something, I make it right. I'm not selling online anymore, but I could if I wanted to. The difference is I know how to make something worth buying.

I think a lot about that person who came back to me about the bracelet. I think about how she was spending money on something I made and I didn't respect that enough to do the work properly. It's a small failure in the scheme of things, but it taught me something important. You don't have to sell something to give it value. But if you do sell it, you have to respect the transaction. You have to care about the person on the other end enough to do the work right, not just well enough.
