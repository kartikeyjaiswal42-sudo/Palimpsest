The shelf was 813.54, American fiction, and I picked it because it was at eye level and because the spines were a color I liked, not because of any plan. I was thirteen, it was the summer before eighth grade, and I had decided, for reasons that made sense only to me at the time, that I was going to read every single book on it in order, left to right, no skipping.

By the eleventh book I hated the project and kept doing it anyway. Some of them were long and slow, about people whose problems felt very far from mine. I remember sitting on the library floor with book fourteen, a six-hundred-page novel about a divorce in Connecticut, thinking I had turned reading, the one thing I actually loved, into a chore I'd assigned myself out of pure stubbornness. I almost quit around book sixteen, told my mom in the car that I was done, that it was a stupid idea anyway, and she just said okay and didn't argue, which annoyed me more than if she had.

I went back the next day only because I didn't want the shelf to win.

Somewhere around book twenty-two, a slim one I'd been dreading because it looked boring on the cover, something shifted. It was about a woman deciding whether to leave a life that looks fine from the outside and isn't, and I read the last forty pages standing up in the aisle because I couldn't make myself slow down. I checked it out again that same week, which I had never done with any book before. I remember standing there afterward, looking at the twenty-some books I still had left, and realizing I wasn't dreading them anymore.

I finished the shelf in October, three months later than I'd planned, forty-one books total. The last one was short and strange and I don't remember liking it much. That wasn't really the point by then.

I still have the list, handwritten, with little check marks, some of them added in pen days or weeks after I actually finished the book, because I'd forgotten to mark it at the time. The paper is soft now from being folded in the same book bag for years. I look at it sometimes and think about book fourteen, sitting on that floor, certain I'd made a mistake, certain the whole idea had been a waste of a summer.
