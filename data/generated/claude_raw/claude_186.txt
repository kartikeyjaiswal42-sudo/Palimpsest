The final project was supposed to be a collaboration. That's what the assignment sheet said. You will work in groups of three to four to create a multimedia presentation about an assigned historical event. The goal is to combine research, design, and presentation skills while learning how to work effectively with your peers.

I was paired with my best friend Maya and two other kids I didn't know as well, and I genuinely thought this would be great. Maya and I had been doing everything together since freshman year. We'd stayed up late countless times talking about college plans and relationships and why our calculus teacher seemed to hate us specifically. Surely we could handle one group project.

I still have the rubric they gave us, printed and marked up with annotations and deadlines. We were assigned the American Civil War, and we split up the work pretty evenly at first. I did the research on causes. Maya did the research on major battles. The other two kids, Jason and Sarah, were supposed to put together the design and presentation.

The presentation was due on a Thursday. By the Tuesday before, I'd sent Jason and Sarah everything I'd found. They sent back drafts of the slides. They were fine. Not perfect, but fine. Then Wednesday rolled around, and Maya called me in what I can only describe as a panic. She said the presentation was terrible, that Sarah had redesigned everything using the wrong color scheme, that it wasn't academically rigorous enough, that we were going to fail. She said we needed to stay up all night and fix it ourselves.

I said I thought it was fine. That it was a group project and we all had to accept some level of compromise. That Jason and Sarah had done their part and we couldn't just rewrite everything the night before without acknowledging that.

Maya said I was being lazy and a bad friend. We fought for twenty minutes on the phone. She said if I wasn't willing to perfect the presentation, then I clearly didn't care about our grade or about our friendship. That ultimatum was the thing that broke something. I realized I was supposed to prioritize her vision of the project over everyone else's work. I was supposed to agree that her standards were the right ones.

I didn't stay up all night. I didn't rewrite the presentation. And we did fine on it anyway. Got a B+, which is solid.

But Maya didn't talk to me for a month after that. When she finally did, it was about the presentation and how it should have been perfect. She couldn't understand why I'd chosen the group over her. I couldn't understand why the group wasn't as important as her.

We're cordial now. We see each other sometimes. But we're not best friends anymore, and we both know it. What I learned from that whole thing is that sometimes the people closest to you are the ones who can't actually let you grow beyond what they've decided you should be. I grew, and Maya couldn't accept that I was becoming someone who made decisions she didn't approve of.
