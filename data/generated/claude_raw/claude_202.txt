My parents put me in the church choir when I was eight. I was good at singing. I had a clear voice that the choir director liked. And for a while, that was enough to make me feel like I belonged there, even though I didn't believe any of it.

I was never really a believer. I went to church because my family went to church, and that seemed like a normal thing to do, and I didn't know how to say no to it. The songs were pretty though. There was something about standing with the other singers and letting the harmonies just lift you up. I liked that part. But the actual believing part—the God part—I couldn't make myself do it. I tried. I really did try. I'd sit in the pews and I'd listen to the sermons and I'd wait for the moment when it would click for me the way it clicked for other people. It never did.

So I faked it. I was in choir for five years. Five years of showing up every Sunday and singing about faith I didn't have with conviction I didn't feel. I got really good at faking it. I could make my voice soar during the uplifting parts. I could make it break emotionally during the sad parts. I knew the theology well enough to talk about it if someone asked me a question. My own parents didn't really know that I was a complete imposter standing in that robes.

The weird part is that I don't think anyone else knew either. The choir director thought I was pious. The other kids thought I was devout. My parents thought they'd raised a daughter with faith. And maybe some part of me was actually faithful, like my voice believed even if my brain didn't. Maybe singing faith is a kind of faith, even if it's not the kind that comes with certainty.

But here's the thing that I still think about: I quit the choir not because I realized I didn't believe, but because I got tired of pretending. I was thirteen and I had this moment of actual anger, not at God or at religion, but at myself for accepting a role that felt dishonest. I walked into rehearsal one day and I just said I was done. The director looked shocked. My parents were disappointed. The other kids probably thought it was weird.

I wish I could say I quit for some noble reason. That I had a crisis of conscience or an epiphany about authenticity. But honestly I just couldn't do it anymore. I couldn't keep producing this exact amount of emotion on command. I couldn't keep being the girl who had faith when I didn't. It felt like lying, not to them, but to myself.

I'm still not religious. I've thought about it a lot since then. Maybe I would have found faith if I'd stuck with it. Maybe belief grows from repetition and I just gave up too early. Or maybe I would have stayed a fraud forever, and getting out was the only honest thing I did there.

I don't think there's a right answer. I just think about that thirteen-year-old version of me walking out of choir practice and deciding that some performances aren't worth maintaining. And I'm glad she did that, even though I can't entirely explain why.
