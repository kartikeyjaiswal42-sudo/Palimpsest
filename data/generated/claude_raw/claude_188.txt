Nadia and I were friends from when we were nine until we were sixteen, and then we did a biology coursework project together and now we are not, and I still do not think I was wrong, which is the part that bothers me.

The project was a four-week investigation on germination rates. Four people, one grade. She and I took it because we were the two who cared, and because doing it together seemed obvious, the way things are obvious when you have known someone since primary.

She missed the second week. Her grandmother had a fall and the family was at the hospital in Leicester and she was there most nights. I knew this. I want to be clear that I knew this, because when people hear the rest they assume I didn't.

I did her section. Then I did the third week's data as well, because she said she would and then didn't, and by then we were four days from submission and I could either do it or hand in something incomplete with my name on it. When she came back she wanted to go over it together and change the analysis, and I said no. I said we were out of time and it was done. She said I had taken it from her. I said she had left it. Neither of us shouted, which somehow made it worse. We submitted. We got a high mark. We have spoken maybe six times since, all of them polite.

Here is my case, which I have rehearsed enough times that I am suspicious of how smooth it has become. Deadlines do not adjust for grief. I had my own grades in it. She had told me twice she would send her part and twice she had not, and I did not ask a third time because asking felt like pressing on a bruise. So I absorbed it. I thought I was being kind.

Here is hers, which I can also make, and which is the reason I am writing this instead of something safer. She never asked me to take it over. She asked for time and I gave her competence instead, and competence was not what she needed. By doing her work silently I made it so that she owed me, and she never agreed to owe me. And when she came back and wanted to be part of it again, I told her the door was closed, and I told her that on the grounds of a deadline that I had decided the shape of by myself.

I think both of those are true. That is the problem. I have gone looking for the version where one of us is simply in the wrong and I cannot find it, and I have stopped expecting to.

What I have taken from it is not a resolution. It is a habit of asking, before I fill a gap somebody has left, whether they have asked me to. Usually they haven't. Usually what I am about to do is for me — for the mark, for the tidiness, for avoiding the conversation where I say I need you to do this and you say you can't.

I still love her. I would still do the work, I think, but I would tell her I was doing it, and I would leave the analysis open until she got back. That is the most honest version I have, and it is not an apology, and I know it.
