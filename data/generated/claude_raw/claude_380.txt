The belief I held, and defended out loud, was that being the only girl on the robotics team did not affect anything, and I changed my mind about it in the middle of a conversation I did not want to be having.

I had said the line many times. A teacher would ask how it was going and I would say fine, honestly fine, they treat me like anyone else. I believed this. I was not performing toughness; I had examined it and concluded it was true, because nobody had ever said anything to me, and I took the absence of insults as the absence of a problem.

Then in my third year a ninth grader named Priya joined, and after about a month she asked me whether I got interrupted a lot. I said no. She said she did. And I said, without thinking about it at all, that she should write down what she wants to say beforehand so she can just read it off, because that is what I do.

I heard myself say it.

I had been doing that for two years. Every meeting. I had built an entire private workaround, an actual system with paper and everything, and I had filed it under being organised rather than under compensating for something, and I had never once put those two facts next to each other until a fourteen-year-old asked me a direct question.

That is what prompted my thinking. Not an incident. The discovery that I had solved a problem I was simultaneously insisting did not exist.

The outcome is smaller than the realisation. I did not confront anyone, because there is nobody to confront; the interrupting is not malicious and the boys doing it would be genuinely upset to hear it described this way. What changed is that I stopped saying the line. When a teacher asks now I say it is mostly good and there are some things about it that are tiring, and I leave it there, which is less satisfying for everyone and closer to true.

I also stopped assuming that my own reports about my life are reliable. I had produced, sincerely, a completely wrong account of my own experience for two years, and I was the only witness. That has made me slower to trust the confident version of anything, including from myself.

Priya writes her sentences down now. She showed me her notebook last week. Hers are longer than mine.
