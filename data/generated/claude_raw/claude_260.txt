My phone went into the canal in Year Ten and my parents said they would replace it in a year. Not as a punishment. They just did not have three hundred pounds sitting around, and I had been told about the canal.

For about five months I was miserable in a specific, boring way. Everything was arranged in a group chat I was not in. I would turn up to things that had moved. Twice I waited outside a cinema for forty minutes.

So I built a system, badly, out of what I had. A pocket diary. I asked people to tell me things out loud and then I wrote them down, times and places, and I read the page before I left the house. I started leaving earlier because I could not text to say I was running late. I learned the actual bus timetable — the 42 and the 17 — because I could not look it up at the stop.

Nobody taught me any of this. There was no method. I just kept running into the same wall until I put something in front of it.

Here is the bit I did not expect. Around March I noticed I was remembering things. Not deliberately. Phone numbers, my friends' timetables, which day Hasan had football. My head had started doing work it had never been asked to do, and it was, apparently, fine at it.

I had assumed the year was a hole I was going to climb out of. That was the thing I changed my mind about, and I can tell you when. It was a Tuesday in April and my friend Dara asked me what time the last 17 went and I answered without thinking about it, and she said, how do you know that, and I said I just do.

I just do. That was the first time it occurred to me that I had gained something rather than lost it.

I got a phone that August. I use it a lot; I am not going to claim I came out the other side purified. But I still keep the diary, still leave early, still know the bus times.

What teaching myself showed me is that I am much more willing to be uncomfortable than I thought, provided nobody has told me the discomfort is good for me. If my parents had taken the phone away on purpose I would have fought it for a year. Because it was just the situation, I had to solve it, and solving it turned out to be interesting.
