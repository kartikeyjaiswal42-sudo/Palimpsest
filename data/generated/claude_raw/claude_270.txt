I was so committed to the pallet furniture phase. Like, embarrassingly committed. I was going to recycle materials responsibly. I was going to be creative and resourceful. I was going to make beautiful rustic pieces that would look amazing in my room and cost basically nothing. My mom thought I was insane. My dad thought I was going through a phase that would pass. But I had watched TikTok videos and Pinterest boards and YouTube tutorials of people building perfect pallet tables, and I was absolutely convinced I could do it too.

The first pallet I found was behind a grocery store. I convinced my brother to help me drag it to our house and I was already planning where it would go. Coffee table. That's what I'd make. I watched three different tutorials and then I grabbed my dad's power tools—without asking, which is maybe the first problem—and I started taking the pallet apart.

It took four hours to disassemble something that probably took fifteen minutes to put together in the factory. My hands hurt. Splinters everywhere. The wood was not as clean as it looked in the videos. There were weird stains and some of the boards were warped. But I was undeterred. I was going to sand it down and make it perfect.

The sanding took two days. My arms hated me. The patio was covered in wood dust. My mom definitely yelled about that. But then I stained it and I have to say it actually looked pretty decent. I was so proud. I put the legs on and I had a functional coffee table. It was crooked. It was uneven. But I had made it and it existed.

I was going to make ten more things. I found pallets literally everywhere once I started looking. Restaurants, stores, parking lots. I started the second project—a headboard for my bed. This was more ambitious. More failure points. And here's the thing I didn't see coming. I was getting tired. And also, I was beginning to realize that recycled pallet furniture is actually kind of ugly. Like, aesthetically speaking, it's a trend that's a trend for a reason and that reason is not that it looks good. It looks like you found wood in an alley and made furniture out of it. Which, to be fair, you did.

Halfway through the second project I just stopped completely. I looked at the half-made headboard sitting in my room and I thought about the coffee table that I'd paid forty dollars in materials to "save money" on. I thought about all the time and the actual effort that had gone into it. I thought about the fact that I could just buy actual real furniture from a store that didn't give me splinters and didn't require five different YouTube tutorials to assemble correctly.

I never finished the headboard. I don't even know where the pieces went. Probably still in our garage somewhere. My coffee table sat in my room for a few months and then I got a real one from IKEA because the pallet table was deeply uncomfortable to put drinks on.

What changed my mind wasn't failure. It was understanding my own motivation. I wasn't actually trying to recycle or be sustainable or creative. I was trying to be the kind of person who did DIY projects. I was performing. And the moment I realized that, it all felt pointless. I'd rather be authentic about not caring than fake caring about pallets.
