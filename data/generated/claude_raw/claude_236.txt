The map is folded wrong. It has been folded wrong since I was eleven, when I refolded it in a hurry on a platform at Jackson Heights and put a permanent diagonal crease through the Bronx. It is the 2014 MTA pocket map, the one where the 7 line is still purple and the Second Avenue stations are not there yet, and it lives in the front pocket of my backpack, where it has softened to the texture of cloth.

I have owned nineteen transit maps. I can tell you why Tokyo's is a mess and why Berlin's is not. What I actually love is the lie in them: the geography is wrong on purpose, the angles are all forty-five degrees, Brooklyn is squeezed and Staten Island is a rumor, and it works, because the map is not answering where things are. It is answering how to get there.

Last year our county announced a redesign of the local bus network and opened a public comment period. I spent four months on a proposal. I made a real map — proper line weights, forty-five-degree angles, a color system that distinguished frequent routes from the ones that run twice a day, which our current map does not do and which is the single most useful thing a rider can know. I had twenty-two pages. I submitted it in October.

I got a form email. Then nothing. In February the new map came out and it was the old map with new colors.

What I did next was drive to the transit authority's public board meeting in March and use the three minutes at the microphone. I was bad at it. I read too fast and I ran out of time in the middle of the frequency argument. But a planner named Ms. Okafor came up afterward, and what she told me was that they had liked the frequency idea and could not use it, because two of the routes I had marked as frequent were about to be cut and the map would have been obsolete in eleven weeks. She said nobody had explained that to me because nobody reads the comments in time to.

I asked if I could see the service change schedule. She emailed it to me. I am on version four now, built around what the network will be in 2027, and I meet with her once a quarter, and it may still go nowhere.

The old map is still in my backpack, still folded wrong. I take it out on long drives. The 7 is still purple on it, and the system it describes stopped existing years ago, and I keep it anyway.
