The server is called Sprout, and it started three years ago as a place for people to post half-finished games from a weekend jam nobody remembers the theme of anymore. There are about four hundred of us now, mostly fourteen to nineteen, scattered across time zones that mean somebody is awake at any hour to answer a question about collision detection or why a sprite keeps flickering at the edge of the screen. I found it the way I imagine most people find the thing that ends up organizing their week: by accident, searching for help with a problem I didn't have the vocabulary to describe yet.

My place in it isn't glamorous. I'm not the best programmer on the server, not close. What I am is the person who answers beginner questions at eleven at night when the actual experts are asleep, because I remember exactly what it felt like to post something and watch it sit there unanswered for six hours. I got good at explaining things badly-explained tutorials had explained badly to me first, which is its own kind of expertise. People started tagging me. Somewhere along the way "ask the person who explains it twice" became a real reputation, small as it is, and I've built almost my whole understanding of who I am as a builder of things around being useful to people who are stuck.

I started learning to code at all because of my cousin Milo, who is eight and was diagnosed with leukemia the year I turned fifteen. He spent most of that year in and out of a hospital forty minutes from us, and then most of the year after that at home but still not really able to go anywhere, still tired in a way that scared everyone even after the doctors said the numbers looked good. I wanted to make him something. Not a card, not a stuffed animal, something he could actually do, so I taught myself to build a small game where you play a knight collecting the exact eight kinds of dinosaurs he was obsessed with that year. It took me four months and it is, by any real standard, not a good game. He has played it probably three hundred times.

My aunt has never liked this. She's Milo's mother, and she loves him more carefully than anyone I know, and she has told me, more than once, in the specific tired voice she uses when she's decided not to raise her voice instead, that a screen is a screen, that what he needs is people in the room with him and not another glowing rectangle, that I am solving my own discomfort with his illness rather than actually helping him. I don't think she's wrong that some of it is about my own discomfort. I don't think she's right that the game is nothing. We have had this conversation four or five times now and it always ends the same way, with both of us a little hurt and neither of us saying anything else about it, because we both know the other one loves him and we can't find the seam where her version of love and mine actually disagree.

I still build Milo new levels. I still don't know how to explain to my aunt why it matters to me the way it does, why sitting in his room while he plays something I made feels like a form of presence and not an escape from it. On Sprout I can explain almost anything to almost anyone. I have never once managed to explain this to her, and I don't think either of us has given up trying, which might be the truest thing I can say about what family actually is.
