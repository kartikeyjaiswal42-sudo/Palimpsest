My brother sat next to my hospital bed for most of a month, and I've never managed to properly thank him for it, and worse, we still disagree, sharply, about what actually happened during that month, which makes the gratitude complicated in a way I didn't expect gratitude to be.

I was in for a bone infection that started as something small and turned, over about a week, into something the doctors took seriously enough to keep me for twenty-nine days, most of it on IV antibiotics, a few of it worse than I want to describe in detail. My brother, Ethan, three years older, was home from his first year of college on a break that was supposed to last ten days and instead lasted the whole month, because he canceled a return flight without telling our parents until after he'd done it, and just stayed, sleeping most nights in the reclining chair by my bed instead of at home twenty minutes away.

He read to me. He brought in a portable speaker and played entire albums we used to listen to as kids, arguing with me about which songs were actually good the way we used to before college put three years and a few hundred miles between how we talked to each other. He missed the first three weeks of his spring semester doing this, which is the part we now disagree about, still, a year and a half later.

I think it was too much. I think he sacrificed something real — a semester's momentum, a housing situation he lost because he wasn't there to sign paperwork on time, a class he had to retake — for a month that, medically, probably would have gone fine either way, with our parents there and him back at school. I've told him this more than once, that I feel guilty every time I think about the semester he lost, and he tells me every time that I'm wrong, that he'd do the exact same thing again without hesitation, that being there mattered more to him than the housing situation or the retaken class, and that my guilt is, in his words, "making his choice about me instead of letting it be his."

We've never actually resolved this. I still think he gave up too much. He still insists he'd do it again. Neither of us has budged, and I don't think either of us is going to, because we're arguing from two different places that don't actually meet in the middle — his is about what he chose to give, mine is about what I think he shouldn't have had to give on my account.

What I've landed on, without fully resolving the argument itself, is that the gratitude and the guilt are somehow the same feeling wearing two different faces, and I've stopped trying to separate them cleanly. I know that a month of someone's presence, freely given even at real cost, is not something you get to fully repay, and I think some part of what motivates me now — showing up for people the way he showed up for me, even when it's inconvenient, even when it costs something real — comes directly out of that month, out of watching what it actually looks like when someone decides you're worth more than their own convenient plans, whether or not I've ever fully made peace with having been the reason he made that choice.
